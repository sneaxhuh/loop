# Loop: three-minute demo

**Pitch:** Your next chapter is on someone else's shelf.

Use the included fictional circle when recording a public demo. Its sixteen book works, confirmed Qloo IDs, and saved cultural rankings are real. Its owners, references attributed to owners, and offered copies are scenario inputs. A screenshot is not evidence that participants preferred a proposal.

## 0:00–0:25 — The problem

“Four readers have books to share. A book I want may belong to someone who does not want mine. Loop finds exchanges involving several owners, using the things people love and the books they actually offer.”

Show the shared shelf and reading circle. Say that this is a fictional demonstration. Open a reader's taste: public music, film, brand, or book references can guide matching across domains.

## 0:25–1:10 — A decision, with evidence

Click **Find a loop**. Show the exchange graph and incoming books. Explain that one agent invokes tools while deterministic code selects valid closed exchanges. Availability, ownership, reading language, read history, declined titles, and the minimum relative rank fit constrain the outcome.

Open **View evidence**. Point to the actual returned ranking and its saved/live source. The rank is relative to the offered pool. It is not the probability that a reader will enjoy the book. The included Gemini comparison uses the same pool and declared references; neither model's predictions are human ground truth.

## 1:10–1:55 — Make it personal

Open **Invite readers**, then preview one reader page. Keep private links and their fragments out of the recorded browser address bar. Show the proposed incoming and outgoing book, the person's references, and **Yes, I'm in** / **Pass & find another way**.

For a live judge interaction, let the judge replace that example reader's favorites with their own public references through **Make it feel more like you**. Confirm search matches explicitly. New inputs use live Qloo. The agent saves a revised proposal or states that no exchange fits. Show what actually changed; some inputs may produce the same handoffs.

## 1:55–2:35 — A consequential repair

Withdraw the reader's outgoing copy, or say “Please withdraw Beloved” in the organizer agent chat. Show the actual tool trace and changed graph. Every involved reader must review the new proposal again. No books have physically moved.

Restore the copy if needed. Passing on an incoming book also produces a valid revised proposal or an explicit no-exchange result; it never quietly substitutes an impermissible book.

## 2:35–3:00 — What has been established

“The captured Qloo experiments show that cultural references and ranking sources change feasible exchanges. We have not yet established that Qloo predicts these people's preferences better than Gemini. The next pilot uses real offered copies, independent pre-reveal rankings, and participant acceptance.”

Download the exchange artifact. Show reproducible local setup and the source repository. Before a public recording, use **Reset the demo** and avoid real reader names, private links, credentials, or personal inventories.

## Recording checklist

- Record the organizer workspace and reader page at desktop resolution; a mobile reader screenshot is included in `docs/assets/`.
- Keep secrets, passwords, private invitation fragments, and real participant information out of the recording.
- If an API call fails, show the preserved proposal and error. Do not present a cached result as a new live call.
- Record actual baseline wins and ties when real reviewers evaluate proposals.
- Warm the free web service before presenting; its first request after idle time can take about a minute.
